## 0. The core idea in one paragraph

Day traders are **"hunters of volatility and managers of risk."** On most days
only **1-2 stocks** move enough to matter. Find the stock with the **most
demand and least supply**: a big % gainer, with breaking news, huge relative
volume and a small float. Skip the first spike, wait for the **first pullback**, and buy
the **first candle to make a new high** with a stop at the pullback low. Take
half off into strength, move the stop to break-even, and exit at the first sign of
weakness. Trade **only in the morning**, **stop after a max loss or 3 losers
in a row**, and give back no more than half the day's profit.

**Priority order:** "The pattern is important, but the quality of the stock is
number one." *First the stock, then the chart.*

## 1. Stock selection: the 5 pillars

| # | Pillar | Rule | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Price** | **$2-$20** (some versions $1-$20) | Most active **$1-$10**, sweet spot **$5-$10**. $1-$5 workable but fewer trades; $10-$20 fewer trades and profits. Reason: a $3→$6 move is easier than $30→$60. |
| 2 | **% change** | **Up ≥10% on the day** | Scanner may start at +4% sorted by gain, but he prefers far bigger (+50% names). Stocks moving within ±4% are "not moving enough." |
| 3 | **Relative volume** | **≥5×** average | Today's volume vs average (sources cite 30-day or 50-day average). Best profits come around 5× and above. |
| 4 | **News catalyst** | **Must have fresh news** | Best: earnings, FDA approval, clinical-trial results, new contracts (pharma/tech/AI). No discernible catalyst means caution. Technical-only breakouts and short squeezes work but are less preferred. |
| 5 | **Float** | **<20M shares**, prefer **<10M** | Tighten to <10M in cold markets. Sub-5M = "rocket fuel." Key idea is **float turnover**: e.g. float 1.5M trading 12M volume = 8× turnover, which often leads to the biggest moves. |

**Bonus signals:** recent IPO, recent reverse split, at/near all-time
highs, unusually low prior-day volume (fresh interest), high short interest.

**Funnel:** thousands of stocks → scanner → ~30-40 → top **5-10 by % gain** → check
news → read chart → usually trade **1-2**.

**Fundamentals check (risk of dilution):** look at recent filings: 10-Q,
10-K, S-1/S-3 (**shelf registration = company can sell shares fast =
dilution risk**), 13D/13G (big holders), Form 4 (insider trades). Low cash,
high liabilities and an active shelf mean the company may dump shares into the spike.

### Avoid
- OTC/penny stocks (pump-and-dump risk).
- Stocks **down ~50%** on the day (bankruptcy-type news).
- **Buyouts/mergers**: price gets pinned and trades sideways like a barcode.
- Complicated merger/dilution headlines.
- **Shorting as a beginner**: losses are unlimited. A short of 1,000 sh at $20 can make at most $20k, but a squeeze to $500 loses about $480k.
- Familiar big names that aren't moving (his early mistake: trading IBM,
  US Steel, AEP out of familiarity when nothing was happening there).
- After-hours trading (thin volume, unreliable).
- Chasing **extended** stocks far from the moving averages.

## 3. Chart setup: "less is more"

- **Timeframes:** 1-minute and 5-minute for entries (10-second chart for micro
  pullbacks), daily for context and levels.
- **EMAs: 9, 20, 200.** 9 = fastest momentum, 20 = trend support, 200 =
  big-picture context. No 50/100 needed.
- **VWAP:** above it = buyers in control (long bias), below it = sellers in
  control (no longs).
- **Volume bars**, colored green/red by candle direction. You "could almost trade
  with volume alone." Want **heavy volume on up-moves and light volume on
  pullbacks**.
- **MACD (standard 12/26/9)** as a **red light / green light**: **only buy when MACD
  is above its signal line (MACD "open").** Most useful in choppy or cold markets.
- **Level 2 + time & sales (tape):** confirmation that buyers are lifting the
  offer (green prints) and that big asks are getting eaten.

## 4. Reading candles: the 3-step method (video 4Pc_von1wS4)

1. **Strip down indicators** to 9/20/200 EMA, VWAP, colored volume, MACD.
2. **Trade only what's obvious:** the stock that is actually moving (5 pillars),
   not what you're familiar with.
3. **Trade the structure with metrics:** let the **first wave** happen → wait for
   the **pullback** → enter when the **next wave confirms** → exit on predefined rules.

**Candle vocabulary:** open/high/low/close; long body = conviction; small
body/**doji/spinning top** = indecision; **long upper wick (shooting star)** =
sellers rejected the high; **hanging man** at highs = warning. A **topping tail** after
a big run plus a red candle on high volume is a first exit warning.

## 5. Entry setups

### 5.1 First pullback / bull flag (the main setup)
- **Context:** stock passes the 5 pillars, has news, has made a strong
  first leg up (the "pole") on heavy volume.
- **Pullback:** 2-5 candles drifting down or sideways on **lighter volume**,
  holding above the 9/20 EMA and **above VWAP**.
- **Entry trigger:** **the first candle to make a new high** (breaks the prior
  candle's high) after the pullback low.
- **Stop:** **just below the low of the pullback.**
- **Best on the first pullback:** "buy the first pullback" on breaking news.
  Later pullbacks are lower quality.
- He trades the **front side** of the move (MACD open, EMAs fanning
  upward). Once EMAs converge and MACD crosses down, it's the **back side**:
  choppy and lower odds, so stop trading it.

### 5.2 Micro pullback
- On very fast movers: a tiny 1-2 candle dip on the **10-second / 1-minute**
  chart during a squeeze.
- Don't necessarily buy the first candle to make a new high. **Wait for
  volume to come in and a break through the high.**
- **Stop = low of the micro pullback.** Very tight risk, so size can be larger.
- Only works in a hot environment. Avoid in chop or weak volume.

### 5.3 Other patterns he uses (from his broader material)
- **Flat-top breakout:** several candles hitting the same high (seller at a level),
  then a break through it on volume. Stop under the consolidation.
- **ABCD:** spike (A→B), pullback that holds a higher low (C), break of B (D) is
  the entry. Stop below C.
- **VWAP break / reclaim:** stock reclaims VWAP on volume after a dip. Stop below
  VWAP.
- **Half/whole-dollar breaks:** $5.00, $5.50, $6.00 act as magnets and
  resistance. Entries often come just before a whole-dollar break.

### 5.4 Post-entry confirmation (exit immediately if missing)
- Price should **move in your favor quickly**: rule of thumb **~10 cents within
  about 1 minute**.
- Tape shows **green prints** (buyers lifting the offer) and **big green
  candles** appear.
- If it stalls, goes red or buyers disappear on Level 2, **get out.** Don't
  wait for the full stop.

## 6. Exits

- **Profit target: at least 2:1 reward-to-risk.** At 2:1 you break even
  at ~33% accuracy. His stated goal is high accuracy *and* 2:1.
- **Scale out:** sell **half** into the first push and **move the stop to break-even**
  on the rest.
- **Exit indicators (sell the rest):**
  - first candle to make a **new low** (especially after a squeeze up);
  - no progress or a stall at highs;
  - buyers disappear on Level 2 / tape turns red;
  - big red candle on high volume, or a long topping tail;
  - MACD crosses down or price loses the 9 EMA / VWAP.
- **Two traps:** selling winners too early (fixed by scaling half) and holding
  losers at full size (fixed by hard stops and exit indicators).

## 7. Risk management and discipline

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Max daily loss** | Set it before the open; stop trading when hit. |
| **3 losers in a row → done for the day** | Prevents the "trading spiral". |
| **Protect half of the day's profit** | Once green, stop if you give back 50% of the day's high P&L. |
| **Start small each day** | **Trade ¼ size until up ~$0.20/share (a "cushion")**, then go full size. He credits this with far fewer red days (45 green days in a row **[unverified]**). |
| **Start with 1 trade a day** | When learning: one setup, one trade. |
| **Scale share size before trade count** | Grow position size gradually; don't increase frequency first. |
| **Only A+ setups** | After a loss, the urge to take B/C setups is the start of the spiral. |
| **Cash account to start** | Avoids margin/PDT problems (check the current PDT rules for your broker). |
| **"Profit trifecta"** | Track **accuracy**, **profit/loss ratio**, **consistency**. Review winning and losing days. |

**The trading spiral:** surprise loss → revenge trade → lower standards
(B/C setups) → more losses → lost confidence. **Fix:** be a "great loser":
cut losses fast and step away.

**Two reasons traders fail:** (1) no defined, repeatable strategy for selection,
entry and exit; (2) no discipline to follow it.

**Practice:** trade in a **simulator** until the strategy is consistently
profitable, then go live small.

## 11. Verified from the video: "Reading Candlestick Charts Was HARD Until I Learned This 3 Step Trick"

Transcribed from the full 48-minute video; frames checked every 20 seconds. Timestamps are mm:ss.

### Step 1: Fewer indicators (02:02-17:45)
- His old chart had "more than 20 different indicators": too many to check, and they give conflicting signals.
  There is no "holy grail" indicator.
- **EMAs 9, 20, 200** (9 gray, 20 blue, 200 magenta). He uses the 9 over the 10 because it's "a little bit
  faster". He doesn't use the 50 or 100. These are periods of candles, so the same on 10-second, 1-minute,
  5-minute and daily charts (17:45).
- **VWAP** (orange): "the equilibrium point for the day." Above = bulls in control, below = bears.
  "As a long bias trader, I'm going to focus on trading when the price is above VWAP." (08:20)
- **Volume bars must be colored** by the candle's open vs close. "You could almost trade exclusively on volume
  bars… to trade without volume bars, you would be almost trading with one eye closed." (10:40)
  - Rising price on **declining** buy volume, then heavy red selling volume = sellers dominate → "at best double
    topping at the high of day… I certainly would not be buying dips or pullbacks." (13:16-13:53)
  - **Rising** volume on the way up and **light** volume on the pullback = buyers in control → "be a buyer near
    the support of the nine moving average… set my stop at the low of this candle… add as the price moves
    higher." (13:58-14:27)
- **MACD:** "I focus on trading when the MACD is above the signal line." Red light / green light. He added it in
  the 2022 bear market and the 2023 rally: "only trading when the MACD is open… greatly reduced the number of false
  breakouts." When it crosses over: "nope I'm not interested anymore." (15:28-17:03)
- Candles are the source; indicators are context that "validate that this is doing what you think." (12:35)

### Step 2: Trade what's obvious (17:57-30:52)
- His beginner mistake was trading familiar names (IBM, US Steel, AEP): "I don't see a gosh darn thing."
- **Relative volume ≥5×, measured against the 50-day average.** His audited metrics: over $10M of his $12.6M
  came from stocks at ≥500% relative volume. He **lost money on lower relative-volume stocks**. (22:03-23:18)
- "High relative volume is a byproduct typically of a news catalyst… you need the catalyst." (23:23)
- Large caps (AEP, TSLA, S&P 500) almost never reach 5× relative volume, so there's nothing to trade.
- He found the pattern after a big loss forced him to review 18 months of metrics. Profits came when:
  1. relative volume ≥5×,
  2. **up more than 10%** ("opening gap being more than 2%, but the instrument being up 10%"),
  3. a **news catalyst**,
  4. **price $2-$20**. (27:08-28:56)
- Workflow: the scanner flags the stock → check the news ("I don't have to be an expert on this stock") → chart →
  "game time". Example: WISA, news "announcing fourth HDTV license with major TV brand", about +$9,600 (≈$9.59
  entry on screen). (29:36-30:20)
- He is right **68.6%** of the time ("wrong 30% of the time and I'm still making money") on **$12.6M** of
  third-party-audited profit. "My results are not typical." (04:03-04:43)

### Step 3: Build a strategy around what works (31:04-48:05)
**Entry: the first candle to make a new high**
- "Let the first wave happen… wait for that first wave to pull back… this is the moment of truth." On the pullback,
  either high-volume selling, MACD crossing over and losing the moving averages (no trade), or "we hold". (32:58-33:14)
- Chasing the first spike: "the candlestick chart's not supporting that entry." (32:39)
- On a rising stock each candle breaks the prior high; on the pullback candles make new lows. "If it keeps making
  new lows, I'm not going to buy… I'm going to wait for the first candle to make a new high." (34:27-34:56)
- "Right after that candle made a low, that's going to be your lowest risk entry… where do you bail out if this
  doesn't work? The low." (36:14-36:24)
- On-screen chart (frame ~32:40): *"Enter as Green Candle Breaks High of Previous Red Candle" · "Stop or Max Loss at
  Low of Pullback" · "Taking profit based on risk vs reward ratio at 1:1, 1:2, 1:3" · "High Volume Green Bars is
  Preferred" · "Light Volume Pullback is Preferred".*
- Always "as long as your indicators are supporting it": volume profile checks out and MACD open. (31:48, 37:44)

**Profit/loss math (36:37-37:25)**
- 10¢ stop, 20¢ target = 2:1. "If you're right even just 33% of the time, you break even… right half the time,
  you're profitable." At 1:1 you need over 50%.
- "It's the combination of profit loss ratio and your accuracy that creates profitability."

**Selling too soon vs holding losers (38:05-40:26, 46:02-47:29)**
- Beginners sell for a tiny 1:1 win and miss a possible 5:1. He was driven by fear of loss on a small account.
- His fix: he reprogrammed his "sell all" hotkey to **sell half**, then moved the stop to **break-even** on the rest
  ("now I have guarantee that this is going to be a winner"). Hotkeys: Shift+1 buy, Ctrl+Z sell all at bid,
  Ctrl+X sell half, Ctrl+C sell a quarter.
- Later he got better at "not selling half until I saw an exit indicator."
- The opposite mistake: holding full size through a rejection ("going down with the ship"). Then average losers
  are big (50¢-$1+) while winners are small. A big seller at a level (e.g. 100,000 shares) can "ruin the chart"
  even when the indicators look good: take the loss, break-even or small win, but **do not keep holding**.
- "Scale out more slowly into the move and… not sell until you have a valid exit indicator." Scaling out is
  right on **big green candles**, where waiting for the first red candle gives back a lot. (41:17-41:35)

**What he must see after entry (41:43-43:16)**
1. **Price moves up: "at least 10 cents within one minute of entering the trade."**
2. **Buyers on time & sales: "green on the tape"** (e.g. 5.50 × 5.60 quote, prints at 5.60, 5.60, 5.70).
3. Large green candles.

**Exit indicators: the inverse (43:22-45:04)**
- No 10¢ move within a minute → out. No buyers / green on the tape → out. No large green candles → out.
  "The next candle goes red and I get right out."
- **"The exit indicator is the first candle that makes a new low."** (40:40) Hold until then.
- At the top: price stops moving, buyers disappear on Level 2, red on the tape, a red candle forms → exit.
- Accuracy is 68%, so "32% of time I'm going to be wrong… I just have to cut my loss quickly."
